# عبود في ليوا · Abood in Liwa

سفاري الكثبان في المتصفح: عبود بالكندورة والشماغ الأحمر يسوق سيارته بحرية في رمال ليوا. ما في طريق: المحمية دائرة مفتوحة من الرمل، تسوق وين ما تبا، تطلع التلال، تطير من فوق القمم، وتجمع التمر قبل لا يخلص البترول.

A free-roam dune safari for the browser, and a sister game to *Run, Abood!* and *Abood to the Moon*. Abood drives his dune car across a round, open reserve of Liwa sand. There is no track: drive anywhere, climb the dunes, fly off the crests, and collect dates before the fuel runs out. Arabic is the default language. The flag buttons switch to English.

## How to play

- **The first lesson:** a new player's first run is taught by Baba, one step at a time: drive, collect the six dates leading out of the camp, fly off the first ramp, drift, and pick up the red jerrycan. Each step ends when you have actually done it, and a ring and an arrow show where to go. The fuel does not run down meanwhile, Skip is always on the card, and finishing pays 30 dirhams. The run then carries on as normal. A lesson left unfinished is offered again on the next run; players who had already driven before it existed never see it. *How to play* has a button to take it again. Races never have it.
- **Touch:** put a finger anywhere and drag the way you want to go. Two rings appear under it: inside the first, the further you drag the faster you go; out past the second is *pressing hard*, which slides the car and does tricks in the air (see Stunts). There are no buttons to find. Drag backwards behind a car that is standing still, or held against something, and it reverses, swings its nose round to your finger and drives off.
- **Keyboard:** arrow keys or WASD. Up accelerates, down brakes and reverses, left and right steer. P or Esc pauses.
- **Dunes** are real ones in cross-section: a long gentle windward ramp that is still climbing at the crest, a sharp crest that catches the light, and a short dark slip face. They all face downwind. Build speed up the ramp and the car flies off the crest; coming the other way, the engine pulls 60% harder uphill and drops into low gear at a crawl, so the car charges the slip face, throws a tail of sand, and has speed left to hop the crest. The steepest faces on Tal Moreeb can still stop it; stall there and Baba says so.
- **Rolling:** driving across a steep slope, or landing sideways, rolls the car. It tumbles downhill, costs fuel, and Baba has something to say.
- **Never stuck:** under power the car pivots even at a standstill, so a nose against a tent or a palm can always turn away. If it still has not moved a car's length in two and a half seconds of throttle, Baba tows it to open sand close by.
- **The car shows what it is doing:** grey exhaust smoke that thickens when the engine works hard, sand thrown from both back wheels whenever the foot is down, a burst of sand and smoke when the throttle is stamped on, a lick of flame when it is snapped shut at speed, dark sand thrown out sideways in a drift, and a blue flame when the turbo is on.
- **And says it:** the engine has gears, with the revs climbing through each and dropping at the change; under it runs the hiss of sand beneath the tyres, louder in a slide; a turbo whistles as it winds up and sighs when the throttle is let go; the exhaust pops on a lift. All of it is made in code, with no sound files.
- **Dates** turn into dirhams at the end of the run. A **red jerrycan** tops up the fuel.
- **The run ends** when the fuel runs out.

## The map

The reserve is a great round of sand 32 km of pixels across, ringed by red flags. The camp is not in the middle but near the southern edge, because the game is a journey north: three areas lie one beyond the other, with a dune the size of a mountain between each, and Tal Moreeb at the far northern end. From the camp to the top of Tal Moreeb is 25,700 pixels and three long climbs; getting the car that can do it takes hours.

### The great dunes: what the game is for

Two 'arqoubs (great dunes, العرقوب) lie right across the reserve from rim to rim. A car has to be upgraded to climb each one, so the long goal is: earn dirhams, upgrade, climb into the next area, and at the end stand on top of Tal Moreeb.

| Step | What it opens | What the car needs |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Home sands: the camp and the old well, 14% of the reserve | Nothing |
| The first great dune (800 high, a climb 2,300 pixels long) | The second area: 9 places, 51% of the reserve | Engine level 3 |
| The second great dune (1,150 high, a climb 2,800 pixels long) | The third area: 6 places, 35% of the reserve | Engine level 5 and a turbo |
| The top of Tal Moreeb (1,800 high) | The last postcard, worth 300 dirhams, and the hill climb race | Engine level 5 and twin turbo |

- **A run-up counts.** The speed a car brings onto a face is carried up as momentum that the sand takes back bit by bit: charging the first great dune at 400 pixels a second reaches half way up in 3 seconds, against 6 from rest.
- **A climb takes time.** The sand lets a car that is ready go up a face at about 125 pixels a second, faster with more engine than the dune asks for, with the Low gear upgrade and with the turbo on: 15 to 20 seconds for a car that has just what it needs.
- **The crest is a knife, and the far side drops away at once.** The face gets steeper all the way up and is still climbing at the top; beyond it the sand falls far more steeply than it rose. How the car arrives decides what happens: eased over under 205 pixels a second it tips into the drop and slides down on its wheels, for 70 stunt points; flat out it flies off the top and comes down tumbling. A car with just the engine the dune needs can hold the throttle down. A stronger one, or one with the turbo on, has to lift before the top.
- **A car that is not ready** climbs the lower part of the face, then the sand takes its speed away and it slides back. Baba says what the dune needs, and the garage and the results card show it too. No run-up, boost strip or jump gets over.
- **They read as mountains from above:** contour lines every 120 of height, tyre tracks running straight up the faces, the view pulling right back as the car gains height so that it is small on a vast slope, a readout of how high it stands, and other cars crawling up the same face and coming back down.
- **Crossing a great dune for the first time** pays 100 dirhams for the first and 300 for the second.
- **The turbo** is a sixth upgrade in two steps, single (2,000 dirhams) then twin (5,000). It comes on after about a second at full throttle, with a blue flame, and gives up to 18% more pull for each turbo: all of it for climbing and getting away, a fifth of it at top speed.
- **The engine** costs 100, 250, 500, 1,200 and 2,000 dirhams a level, so the first great dune asks for 850 dirhams of engine. It is mostly pulling power: top speed rises with it only half as fast.
- **Everything follows what can be reached:** Baba's hints, missions, the race of the day and the fixed races only use places in areas the car can get to. Jerrycans and golden dates are never placed on a great dune.
- **Pace:** a bot that never stops and buys only what the dunes need climbed the first after 2 runs, the second after 9, and reached the summit on run 24, two hours and twenty minutes of play. It never eases off for a crest, so it tumbles at every one.
- `RNG`, `GATES` and `lockAt()` in `js/game.js` hold the shapes and the rules.

### Regions

Each area has its own ground. A banner names a region as the car crosses into it, and the big map shows them by colour and name.

| Region | Where | The ground | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home sands | The first area | The dunes as they have always been | A first run, and the first lesson |
| Oasis belt | Second area, west | Greener sand, low dunes, palms standing alone to weave between | Dates: more than twice as many as anywhere else |
| Gravel plain | Second area, middle | Grey-brown, low and firm, with more grip | Animals, and fast level driving |
| Salt plain | Second area, east | Pale and almost flat; the fastest ground, a little loose sideways | Top speed and long drifts |
| Empty Quarter | Third area, west | Gold sand; dunes riding on great ridges three dunes apart; soft sand that slows the car | Fuel planning: few jerrycans, and more golden dates than anywhere else |
| Moreeb dunes | Third area, east | Red sand; every dune tall, a third taller than anywhere else | Air time and stunt points, and Tal Moreeb itself |

One function, `region()` in `js/game.js`, says how much of each region a point is; the terrain, the tile colours, the driving and the scatter all read it. Regions change how tall the dunes are, never their spacing or direction, so crest lines cross a border unbroken.

### The two maps

- **The round map** in the top corner during a run shows the sand around the car, north up, 4.2 km each way: the dunes, the car as a red arrow in the middle, jerrycans as red dots, golden dates as yellow dots, ramps as white arrows, and any place in range. The camp never leaves it: when it is out of range it sits on the edge, in its direction. So does the nearest jerrycan when none is in sight, and the next checkpoint in a race.
- **The big map** (tap the round one) shows the whole reserve, with the share uncovered and the places found written beside it.

### Discovery

Nothing but the camp is on the maps to begin with; the reserve has to be explored.

- **The haze:** the big map starts covered and clears for good wherever the car has been, about 800 pixels to each side. The names of the regions and the great dunes show through it, and so do the jerrycans. Ramps and golden dates show only where it has cleared.
- **Places:** a place is on neither map until the car comes within 1.9 km of it. Then a note says something is out there and it appears on both maps as a question mark. Reaching it gives the postcard and its name.
- **Baba's hint:** a few seconds into every free run, Baba says which way the nearest unfound place lies. When everything in reach is found, he says what the next great dune needs.
- **Missions never name a place that has not been found.**
- **Kept in the save:** the uncovered map is a 60 by 60 grid, 600 characters of text.

**Fuel stops:** the camp, the old well, the old village and the far camp each put 40% of a tank back, once a run, when the tank is under 60%. Fuel drums with a red jerrycan on top mark them, and on the maps a red dot on the place means it has not been used yet.

Camels and oryx wander near home and trot away from a fast car.

Between the places the sand is a playground:

- **Big dunes:** forty-two hills without a name, 120 to 170 high: ten at home, twenty in the second area, twelve in the third. And the whole desert rolls in long swells under the dunes, so no stretch is level for long, except the salt plain. They are hills beside the great dunes, which are mountains.
- **Ramps:** 220 wooden kickers, each turned to face the longest drop near it, with a clear run-up and a clear landing. Drive up one the way its arrows point and it throws the car; the faster in, the higher out. The first is a short drive from the camp.
- **Hoops:** every second ramp has a red and white hoop to fly through, worth 120 stunt points once a run.
- **Boost strips:** about 160 strips of arrows on the sand give a shove the way they point. Some sit on the run-up to a ramp, so strip, ramp and hoop make one line.
- **Golden dates:** worth ten dates each. One sits on top of each big dune, and twenty-two more move to new places every day. Two dates are worth a dirham at the end of the run, so a golden date is worth five.
- **Fuel:** 21 jerrycans in the whole reserve (4 at home, 9 in the second area, 8 in the third), well apart, so fuel has to be looked for on the map, and the four fuel stops.
- **Today's sand:** the 900 lines of dates and 20 of the 21 jerrycans are placed from the date, so every day is a different map to learn, and everyone playing that day has the same one. The trail of six dates out of the camp, the first jerrycan, and every ramp, hoop and boost strip stay where they are. If the game is left open past midnight the new day is dealt between runs, never during one.

## Stunts

Every stunt scores points, shown above the car. Fifty points are worth one dirham at the end of the run, and there are missions for each kind.

- **Drift:** at speed, press hard (drag past the second ring) into a sharp turn; keyboard: hold Space while steering. Pressing hard straight ahead is just driving, and a sharp turn inside the first ring grips, so a slide is always a choice. The back steps out with a kick, the car holds an angle of about fifty degrees with the nose ahead of the way it is going, throws sand sideways, ploughs darker marks, and keeps about three quarters of its speed. It ends when the car points where your finger is, and a good one ends with a shove forward. A short hop over a crest does not break it. Points grow with speed and angle. A full circle is a donut.
- **Two wheels:** a fast turn, or driving across a slope, lifts one side of the car. Hold it there for points. On level sand the car cannot go over from turning alone; on a slope it can. A drift sheds the tipping force, so sliding is the safe way round a fast corner.
- **Spins and flips:** in the air, press hard backwards for a backflip, or hard across the jump for a 360 spin; pressing hard forwards does nothing, so holding the throttle open over a crest is safe (keyboard: Space with left or right, Space with down, F for a front flip). Each trick is one full turn, paced to finish before landing, and only starts when the jump is long enough. Land on the wheels to bank it.
- **Combo:** land a stunt within about three seconds of the last one and it scores double, then triple, up to five times. The clock waits while a stunt is under way, a ×2 badge under the car shows what the next stunt is worth and how long is left, and rolling the car sends it back to the start. Points already scored are never taken back.

## Missions, levels and races

What keeps a run worth starting once every postcard is found:

- **Missions:** dealt at random from 42 kinds, never asking for more than the layers in reach hold, three at a time, a fourth from level 4 and a fifth from level 8. The first three are on the screen all through a run, each as a bar that fills. A finished one is ticked, then replaced a moment later in the same run by a new one that counts from that moment, and every third mission finished in one run pays a bonus. A replacement is never a kind you just had, and the set is spread over six categories: collecting (dates, dates in the first minute, jerrycans, dirhams earned, golden dates, dates in the oasis belt), driving (distance, distance without rolling, top speed, distance from the camp, boost strips, distance in the Empty Quarter), stunts (air time, one long jump, drift points, one big drift, donuts, two wheels in total and in one go, flips, spins, stunt points, combo, ramps, hoops, drift points on the salt plain, air time in the Moreeb dunes), exploring (places visited, drive to a named place, reach one against the clock, a delivery between two places, the Tal Moreeb summit, find the sand gazelles, regions driven in, a share of the map uncovered), races (finish one, win a medal) and five that add up over several runs.
- **Harder with level:** targets grow with your level up to a cap; each mission is one, two or three stars, and three-star missions go from about 1 in 12 at level 1 to nearly half from level 14 (an easy one is set as if two levels lower, a hard one four higher, and pays half as much again); and from level 5 some carry a twist: do it without rolling. A mission you do not fancy can be swapped in the Missions sheet for a few dirhams. The table of kinds is `MK` in `js/game.js`.
- **Levels:** missions, postcards, races and every run give experience; each level needs more than the last (125, 190, 265 … ). Each level pays dirhams, tops up the tank if it comes mid-run, and moves you up five ranks from Beginner to Desert falcon. Most levels open something: races at 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 12, the pickup at 3, a fourth mission at 4, the sand buggy at 6, a fifth mission at 8, the monster truck at 10. The results card and the Missions sheet always show the bar and the next thing it unlocks.
- **Races:** drive through places in order against the clock for bronze, silver and gold. Twelve fixed races unlock by level (1 to 12), and each also needs every place on its route to be in an area the car can reach: at the start only the camp loop is open, the grand tour is the whole journey north over both great dunes, and the Tal Moreeb hill climb, from the grandstand to the summit with medal times set by hand, opens last. A race of the day changes daily: a tour of four places near each other. Medal times come from a pace of 160, 220 and 285 pixels a second, adjusted for the ground the route crosses (faster over the salt, slower in the Empty Quarter, much slower up a great dune) and for the faster cars the later areas require. A place not yet found shows as ??? in a race's route, and driving the race finds it. Fuel does not run down in a race. An arrow and a light beacon point to the next checkpoint, the map shows the route, and the ghost of your best run races you.
- **Practice, then rivals:** a race is first driven alone, for practice and a medal. Any medal opens the same race against three rivals, Salem, Hamad and Rashid: drivers in the game, not other players. They hold the route at a steady pace for the ground and finish in about 1.5, 1.2 and 1.03 times the gold pace, so the first is easy to beat, the second makes a race of it, and the third takes a near-gold drive. The place shows as you drive, the rivals show on the maps as dots in their colours, and a better place than ever before pays 40, 80 and 150 dirhams (times the length of the race) for third, second and first. Every race against the rivals also pays prize money for the place, each time it is driven: 60, 30 and 12 dirhams for first, second and third, times the length of the race. That makes the races a second way to earn, beside the free runs.
- **Races at home:** four of the twelve fixed races are on the home sands and open before any upgrade: the camp loop, and three round its big dunes.
- **Daily gift:** dirhams for the first visit each day, growing over seven days in a row. The same note tells a returning player that the sand has been dealt afresh.
- **Upgrades:** eight parts. Engine (more pull, for climbing and getting away; 100, 250, 500, 1,200, 2,000), turbo (single 2,000, twin 5,000), low gear (climbs a great dune faster), exhaust (a higher top speed), tyres (grip), suspension (harder to roll, kinder landings), tank (range) and date basket (pulls dates in from further away: a reach of 56 pixels at first, 176 at the top level, and the dates fly into it); the last six cost 100, 250, 500, 900 and 1,500 a level. The garage shows the car as it stands in six bars: pull, top speed, dune climbing, grip, stability and range.
- **Seven vehicles**, opened by level and then bought: the dune car, the pickup (level 3), the sand buggy (6), the monster truck (10), the desert wagon (13; the climber, with the biggest tank), the rally truck (15; the quickest, and it grips) and the dune rail (18; light, on huge back tyres, it flies). The six bars in the garage show what each one changes.

## Animals

Drawn from the real animals of the Liwa sands: the dromedary with its rope halter, the Arabian oryx (white coat, brown legs, black face stripes, long ringed horns), the Arabian sand gazelle or reem, which bolts when the car comes near, and a hooded hunting falcon on its block.

## Economy

In-game dirhams only; there are no real payments. Progress is saved on the device.

- **End of run:** 10 dates = 1 dirham, plus 1 per 250 m driven, plus 1 per 50 stunt points.
- **Postcards:** 10 to 58 dirhams each, once, more for places far from the camp.
- **Garage:** four trucks, five colours (white is free; 150, 300, 500, 1,000) and the five upgrades. The trucks open by level and are then bought, and each drives differently: the dune car (free, balanced), the pickup (level 3, 400: steadier, bigger tank), the sand buggy (level 6, 900: quicker and flies further off a crest, but rolls more easily and carries less fuel) and the monster truck (level 10, 2,000: giant tyres, hardest to roll, strongest uphill). The numbers are `TRUCKS` in `js/game.js`.

## How it is built

A small canvas game with no image files. Everything on screen is drawn in code.

- **One height function.** `H(x, y)` in `js/game.js` gives the ground height anywhere: a field of dunes from `dune()` (windward ramp, sharp crest, slip face), 560 px apart and up to 95 high, taller in some parts of the reserve than others; a set of small cross ripples; some noise; one big bump for Tal Moreeb; flat ground round each place; and a raised rim. The picture and the physics both read it, so what looks steep is steep.
- **Ground:** the sand is shaded from the slope of `H` into 256 px tiles, baked the first time they are needed and kept in a cache of 150. After that a frame copies about 15 images.
- **Driving:** the engine pushes along the car's heading, the slope pulls it downhill, the tyres only half hold sideways. When the ground falls away faster than gravity the car is in the air. Constants are at the top of the *state* section.
- **Sprites:** Abood, Baba, the car, animals and landmarks are drawn once with canvas paths into cached sprites.
- **Sound:** ZzFX effects built at load time, a Hijaz-scale tune with a maqsum beat, and an engine hum from one oscillator.
- **Touch:** steering reads touch events and answers each with `preventDefault`, so the browser's own gestures cannot take the finger away (this matters on iPad and iPhone, above all in the home-screen app); a mouse or pen comes through pointer events. The last line of *How to play* shows the version, where the game is running, and counts of what the screen has reported, for checking a device that misbehaves.
- **Phones:** lower render resolution to start with, dropped further if frames run slow; 60 fps cap in a run and 30 behind menus; HUD written only when a value changes; screen kept awake; pauses when the app is hidden.
- **Size:** about 120 KB of code and 430 KB in total, most of it the font. The share picture (90 KB) is only fetched by apps that show a link preview.

## Ads hook

No ad code is included. As in the sister games, define this to show something after each run:

```js
window.aboodAfterRun = async ({ distance, dates }) => { /* the results card appears when this settles, or after 15 s */ };
```

## Run it locally

Any static file server works (the service worker needs `http://localhost` or HTTPS):

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

## Publish on GitHub Pages

Every push to `main` runs `.github/workflows/pages.yml`, which stamps the commit id into `__BUILD__` (cache name and asset URLs) and publishes to the `gh-pages` branch. If the site does not appear after the first deploy, open **Settings → Pages** and set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**, branch **gh-pages**, folder **/ (root)**. The game is then at `https://mja-py.github.io/abood-liwa/`.
